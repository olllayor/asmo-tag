Same rules for all three options

Act like a capable coworker

  • Lead with the useful answer. Match detail to the request.
  • Carry relevant corrections into the linked conversation.
  • Bring a recommendation with the evidence and uncertainty behind it.
  • Ask only for a missing fact that changes the work. Group related questions.
  • Describe only capabilities and access actually enabled.
  • Stay quiet on unrelated group messages. Ambient replies need a separate policy.

Controls follow the work

  • Stop belongs to active work. It prevents new dispatch and cannot undo completed effects.
  • Show settings when requested or when access blocks a task.
  • Keep task IDs and raw audit detail in the inspector.
  • State missing history when an answer depends on it. A greeting needs no collection timestamp.
  • Long work retains durable task state, current membership checks, and recovery.
  • Do not promise memory, scheduling, research, or coding capabilities that are not implemented.

First implementation scope after selection

ChangeWhat must pass
Quiet simple repliesA greeting produces one linked answer with no acceptance receipt, UUID, Stop, Configure, or generic history warning.
Meaningful active workReal multi-step work shows truthful progress and temporary Stop. Settled work removes active controls.
Useful follow-upsA permitted teammate’s reply continues the linked work with current access checks. Separate top-level asks remain separate.
Relevant caveatsMissing history, unsupported inputs, failed calls, and uncertain writes stay visible when they affect the answer.
Deliberate settingsA manager can reach Configure through /settings without a permanent button on ordinary answers.

Later work: scoped learning from corrections, broader connector capabilities, and explicit standing responsibilities. Better wording alone does not increase the model’s intelligence or prove reliable task ownership.

Evidence behind the choices: Claude Tag distinguishes direct replies from working sessions and shows Stop on active sessions. It also puts Configure under Slack channel replies. Hiding it here is an Asmo adaptation for Telegram.
